# Initiative: Computational Advisor Tracking and Communication Initiative 

## Scope

This initiative reviews the current modes of communication and project updates between computational advisors and the HAAG Leadership team. The initiative also incorporates mechanisms to monitor the consistency and thoroughness with which computational advisors document researcher performance.

## Current State

Currently, researchers submit a weekly report through which they communicate their weekly progress to their advisors. Computational advisors then conduct a HAAG administered weekly survey assessing individual researchers' progress and qualitative observations. Although this survey gives HAAG leadership a sense of how each team is progressing, it is too broad and relatively unclear how thoroughly the computational advisors are reviewing weekly reports and tracking progress/contributions from each researcher. 

I have spoken to a few computational advisors regarding the current process. The feedback that I have gotten is that although the current survey is short and quick to fill out, it has its limitations. As mentioned earlier, the survey is quite broad. The layout is listed below:

* Advisor Name – first question (text entry).
* Lab Selection – multiple-choice question
* Project Selection – displayed conditionally based on selected Lab.
* Performance Checks – short multiple-choice questions rating students (Good / Needs Improvement / Poor).
* Overall Team Status – one question with options:
    * On Track 
    * Needs Improvement
    * Blocked
* Open-Ended Comments – optional question for additional notes.

Computational advisors face confusion when conducting performance. For instance, they do not know which category to select if a researcher has stopped putting in effort altogether. There is no standard procedure of how thoroughly HAAG admin review advisors' comments in the open ended comments section. Lastly, there is no real confirmation of if computational advisors are thoroughly reviewing researchers' weekly reports to the point that they can justly grade the researchers using this survey. 

## Problem Statement

​
There is a need to more closely monitor the consistency and thoroughness of computational advisors’ documentation of researcher performance. Further, there is a need to improve communication (regarding updates/needs/questions) between HAAG leadership and computational advisors that is well-organized and documented in one place.​
​

## Needs Statement

If there is improved clarity with which computational advisors communicate their researchers' progress, then each research project will be more likely to stay on track and meet each team's end of semester goals. This is to be done through improving the computational advisors' workflow to stay concise yet to also outline detailed individual researcher progress. 

## Proposal
I plan on Changing the Weekly check in survey in following ways:​
First, it will Include more categories than "good", "needs improvement", and "poor", such as a "did not participate" option​. This will trigger a notification to Slack to have the HAAG staff check in with that specific researcher to send them reminders. 
Second, there will be  a text box for computational advisors to write feedback or updates for each researcher. Similar to the open comments field, this will be optional. However, the goal is to integrate the survey results into the researchers' weekly report tracker so that all comments/updates from researchers and computational advisors are in a centralized location.  
Lastly, I will add a brief mandatory text box outlining where the research group is at in terms of progress and relative to their long term goal​.

## Procedure

Below is a brief procedure that walks computational advisors through their tasks for involvement in HAAG. This procedure outlines the current weekly 

1. Review final HAAG Enrollment sheet to determine names of faculty, computational advisors, and project names. 

2. Update the revised survey with the relevant information. 

3.  The main tasks for computational advisors is to mentor their researchers and assess their students' work on a weekly basis. The advisors will fill out the Weekly Check-in Survey to do so, which is sent to them via a Slack notification every week. 

4. Slack should notify the HAAG Admin if there are researchers that did not participate. This will trigger an automatic message from the admin to that specified researcher asking them for updates on their situation.

## Future Work - Evaluation Criteria

Aside from implementing the survey next semester (Summer 2026), I would also like to create an evaluation pipeline to track how effective the new survey procedure is. 

Two indicators will be used to evaluate impact.

1. Qualitative results
   * Feedback from computational advisors that use the old and new survey
   * Feedback from HAAG leadership members regarding the new survey and how effective survey results are in helping their understanding of each project's progress. 

2. Quantitative results
   * Simple, easy to follow survey of how computational advisors and HAAG leadership feel about the current process. After protocol implementation, will resend the survey and compare the results. 


The initiative will be considered directionally effective if:

* Computational advisors can clearly communicate if they are taking steps to reach the long-term team goals
* Computational advisors have a very clear idea of what each researcher is working on 
* HAAG leadership members feel confident that each team is on track. 
* Less ambiguity between computational advisors and HAAG leadership.
